# Truepost market-entry strategy — 09/20/2026

## Executive conclusion

Truepost should not try to become “QuickBooks, TaxDome, Wave, Karbon, Canopy, and a tax-preparation system” in the next 30–60 days. That would create a broad, unverified product and lose the trust-sensitive workflows that make accounting software valuable.

It can become a compelling, premium practice operating system if it does three things exceptionally well:

1. makes messy evidence and bank activity reach an accountant-reviewed, defensible cash P&L faster than any combination of Wave/Excel/email;
2. runs the accounting firm’s next action—not a generic CRM board—from recurring close through client follow-up, review, and tax-ready handoff; and
3. connects cleanly to the systems firms cannot prudently abandon yet, especially QuickBooks and professional tax software.

The market is not empty. TaxDome, Canopy, Karbon, Financial Cents, QuickBooks, Wave, and tax-advisory platforms each already solve meaningful parts of this job. The commercial opening is the handoff between those systems: raw evidence, uncertain transactions, client follow-up, accountant judgment, and tax readiness.

## The important correction to the strategy

Basic client portals, e-signatures, workflows, reminders, mobile receipt capture, bank feeds, AI categorization, billing, and dashboards are **table stakes**, not differentiators. QuickBooks now markets AI-driven transaction context and client questions; TaxDome offers tax-return delivery, reminders, portal/mobile access, signatures, and KBA; Canopy, Karbon, and Financial Cents each sell variations of portal + workflow + billing.

The way to beat them is not a longer feature menu. It is a trusted, measurable flow with fewer places for work to disappear.

## Where Truepost can be structurally better

This is the side of the market that matters commercially. It is not enough to say that competing products have a feature; the question is whether the feature is affordable, usable at firm volume, connected to the rest of the work, and defensible when something goes wrong.

### The product promise to earn

> **No mystery work. No surprise credits. No lost receipt. No silent classification. No second system required to understand why the books say what they say.**

That is a differentiated promise because it is operational, testable, and grounded in the professional's daily stress—not generic “AI-powered” language.

## What should be proprietary versus integrated

**Build and own:** evidence model, exception logic, policy/rule versioning, close-packet completeness rules, review ergonomics, work orchestration, client-request state, audit history, reporting/export format, and the migration proof process. Those become stronger with every accepted/declined decision and are difficult to copy without the whole workflow.

**Integrate, do not imitate:** bank aggregation, payment processing, government e-file, identity/KBA verification, calendar/email delivery, and SMS carriage. These are regulated or network-dependent services. The proprietary value is the workflow around them, with failover and auditability—not pretending to be Plaid, Stripe, the IRS, or an identity bureau.

## The Truepost wedge

### 1. Evidence-to-close, not another dashboard

The central object should be a **Close Packet** for each client/month:

- imported bank activity, with a declared source and duplicate prevention;
- every receipt linked to the transaction or explicitly marked unmatched;
- every classification marked as automated suggestion, firm rule, or accountant decision;
- business/personal/transfer disposition visible before it affects the P&L;
- an exception inventory: missing receipt, low-confidence extraction, duplicate, currency conflict, uncategorized item, unmatched transfer, and client response due;
- a completeness gate that prevents “final” status while material exceptions remain;
- a review/approval history; and
- a stable export package for the tax preparer.

This is more valuable than an AI score because it makes the professional's judgment visible and repeatable. It is also compatible with QuickBooks rather than requiring a risky rip-and-replace.

### 2. Migration as a product, not onboarding labor

The immediate sales wedge against Wave and spreadsheet firms is a **Migration Proof Run**:

1. accept Wave CSV / bank statement / receipt archive;
2. map columns and categories transparently;
3. produce a reconciliation report comparing source rows, accepted rows, excluded rows, duplicates, and unresolved exceptions;
4. require a practitioner sign-off before the first period is treated as closed; and
5. export a clean archive so the customer never feels trapped.

The product wins trust when it shows what it did *not* understand. “No silent assumptions” is a better commercial promise than “AI did your books.”

### 3. Work is the unit of automation

Firms do not need a generic chat agent. They need predictable recurring delivery:

- monthly bookkeeping close;
- quarterly estimate package;
- tax organizer and return delivery;
- 1099/W-9 collection;
- year-end evidence chase; and
- advisory review.

Each service template needs owners, due dates, prerequisites, client-facing requests, automatic-but-reversible reminders, escalation, and an audit trail. Work should advance when evidence is accepted—not merely when a user clicks a pipeline stage.

### 4. Human approval is the premium experience

The desired overnight experience is not an autonomous system making tax decisions. It is a concise morning **Ready for Review** briefing:

- actions completed automatically and why;
- high-confidence items ready for batch approval;
- ambiguous items grouped by client and dollar impact;
- prepared—but unsent—client reminders and email drafts;
- work blocked by client action; and
- changes requiring licensed-practitioner judgment.

That gives Phyllis leverage while preserving control, insurance defensibility, and client trust.

## 30-day commercial readiness plan

### Gate 0: truth, safety, and product integrity (first 7 days)

These are prerequisites, not optional polish:

1. Remove all fabricated “live” financial values, activity, and integration-status copy.
2. Secure or disable the public inbound-SMS webhook until carrier signature verification, media-host validation, bounds, and audit tests exist.
3. Configure and real-test every service that is marketed: Turnstile, owner authentication, sender domain, provider webhooks, and observability. Do not market code paths as active integrations.
4. Rename the “IRS DIF” feature as an internal risk heuristic. Include sources, assumptions, version, and practitioner sign-off. Never present it as the IRS algorithm or an audit probability.
5. Execute a disposable, production-like acceptance run: actual Wave export, receipt batch, bank statement containing personal and business rows, failure/retry path, P&L, and export. Preserve the result as a release artifact.

### Gate 1: core workflow that wins the pilot (days 8–21)

1. **Close Packet / exception console.** One source of truth for every unresolved item and every P&L-inclusion decision.
2. **Reliable Wave/CSV migration wizard.** Import preview, repeatable mappings, validation report, and rollback—not just a file upload.
3. **Recurring service templates.** Start with Monthly Bookkeeping, Individual Tax Return, S-Corp/Business Return, and 1099 season. Include defaults but permit a firm override.
4. **Client-request engine.** Secure magic links, response deadlines, follow-ups, delivery receipts, re-open behavior, and a visible “waiting on client” queue.
5. **Tax-prep handoff.** A validated copy/export workpaper for My Tax Prep Office, explicitly labelled as manual entry. Do not suggest a direct integration until one is tested.
6. **Audit-quality reporting.** Cash basis noted everywhere; source links and exclusions are visible; a report cannot be called complete if material exceptions remain.

### Gate 2: purchase-critical parity (days 22–45)

1. **Bank connectivity, safely.** Implement one provider end to end (Plaid *or* Teller), account linking, encrypted token storage, incremental sync, disconnect/reconnect handling, webhook verification, duplicate/idempotency rules, and CSV fallback. It does not count until it runs against a real test institution.
2. **Firm operations.** Recurring work, team assignment, service-template dependencies, workload/deadline view, and an immutable change history.
3. **Branded client delivery.** Branded portal/domain, secure document exchange, accessible mobile upload, request progress, and a no-dead-end recovery path.
4. **Billing only if payment collection is in launch scope.** Stripe/CPACharge-style integration requires webhooks, reconciliation, refunds/disputes, audit history, and tax/legal review. Do not rush it merely to claim parity.
5. **Ordinary document e-sign.** Make ordinary signing excellent first: templates, multiple signers, reminders, revocation/voiding, certificate download, and a verified completion trail. Use a proven compliant identity/KBA partner if Form 8879 is a launch requirement.

### Gate 3: differentiators (days 46–60)

1. **Morning Review Briefing.** A scheduled job produces drafts and queues—never silent action—for pending evidence, client reminders, close blockers, and high-confidence suggested approvals.
2. **Rulebook with evidence.** Vendor and disposition policies are versioned, tested against historical examples, scoped to a client/firm, approved by a human, and reversible.
3. **Multi-entity transfer proof.** Match both sides only when date/amount/entity evidence supports it; create an exception when it does not.
4. **Clean Exit Archive.** A downloadable data package with raw uploads, bank source files, classifications, audit history, signed records, and a human-readable index.
5. **Limited QBO bridge.** Import/export and side-by-side reconciliation first. Do not promise a universal sync until conflict resolution, retry behavior, and full auditability have been proven.

## What must not be built in the next 60 days

- Payroll, payroll-tax filing, merchant acquiring, ACH origination, or holding customer funds.
- An independent federal/state return-calculation and IRS e-file engine.
- Inventory, manufacturing, point-of-sale, broad ERP, or a generic CRM.
- Autonomous tax advice or automatic filing decisions.
- “AI agents” that send financial or tax-sensitive communications without a configured sender, review policy, durable job record, and approval boundary.

Those systems carry regulatory, reconciliation, support, and operational burdens that would delay the actual wedge.

## Defensible launch position

Use this only after Gate 0 and Gate 1 are verified:

> Truepost is the evidence-to-tax-readiness workspace for independent accounting firms. It turns receipt archives and bank activity into a reviewable, traceable monthly close—while keeping every exception, client request, and preparer decision in one place.

Do **not** claim today:

- “replaces QuickBooks completely”;
- “IRS DIF audit score” or “audit-proof”;
- “fully autonomous bookkeeping”;
- “live bank feeds,” “SMS,” “Turnstile,” “Telegram,” “Gmail,” or “QuickBooks integration” until configured and exercised;
- “Form 8879 compliant” without the appropriate delivery/identity process; or
- a measured number of hours/dollars saved before a documented pilot establishes it.

## Launch scorecard

The product is commercially credible for a one-firm pilot only when all are true:

| Criterion | Evidence required |
|---|---|
| Source truth | No fake figures/statuses; every number links to source data or is visibly unavailable. |
| Core close | A representative client period reaches a reconciled cash P&L with all material exceptions visibly resolved or accepted. |
| Recovery | Bad receipt, duplicated import, bank disconnect, and client non-response each have a tested path. |
| Security | Auth, tenant isolation, external webhooks, secret configuration, and audit logging are tested in the deployed environment. |
| Client experience | A client completes a mobile evidence request and ordinary signature request without staff intervention. |
| Operational readiness | Alerts and support queues run through real configured channels, with an owner and response expectation. |
| Commercial proof | Phyllis validates one monthly close and provides actual time, error, and client-friction observations. |

## The honest answer

Yes, Truepost can be exceptional. The moat is achievable—but it will come from a reliable, evidence-first close system with excellent human control, not from an inflated list of partially configured integrations. Win the first firm hard, measure the result, then expand from the proof.
